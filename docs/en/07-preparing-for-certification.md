## Turn each module into a skill

A useful revision target is something you can do with a fresh example, rather than a paragraph you can repeat.

| Module | Skill to demonstrate | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Place a hand-off in a business area and flow | A justified reading of a new event |
| 2 | Preserve object meaning across that hand-off | Named objects and labelled links |
| 3 | Assign responsibilities and a decision owner | Inputs, outputs and ownership per block |
| 4 | Explain an interface independently of its implementation | A proposed contract and stated assumptions |
| 5 | Separate owned, exposed and consumed responsibilities | A component boundary and a rejected dependency |
| 6 | Trace repair evidence into an adjustment | Replay diagnostics and an explanation of pending work |

These are learning targets for this repository, not official competence labels. Start with the mobile-plan story, then change one condition: replace a SIM, lose a restoration message or deliver an adjustment twice. Explain what changes and what must remain linked.

## Assess yourself with evidence

Use [the original question bank](../../labs/07-self-test/README.md). Answer before revealing the suggested correction. Every item names its module and section. Read that section when your explanation disagrees with the correction; do not memorise the correction as a stand-alone slogan.

Mark each response as explained unaided, explained after rereading, or still unclear. Keep a short private note of the missing distinction. For example, “accepted is not ready” should lead to a concrete account of which evidence the update desk waits for. The script does not grade your prose or calculate an exam score.

After a lab failure, explain the violated rule before changing the input. A passing test is useful evidence about the exercise, but your explanation should also state its limit. Remembering a dependency name is different from proving a remote service works.

## Space the revision

An example schedule is an initial session, another two days later, then a week later. These intervals are a personal planning suggestion, not a verified optimal schedule. Adjust them according to what remains unclear. Begin each session from memory, compare with the relevant section and revisit uncertain answers in the next session.

Mix modules when you repeat a scenario. Ask which object crosses the boundary, which block owns the decision and which contract describes the exchange. Then add a duplicate or delay. This keeps a correct definition attached to a practical consequence.

## Exercise

Choose two weak skills from your private notes. Draw one hand-off, answer five randomly selected questions and run the relevant lab. Explain one alternative design and one limit of the evidence you collected. On the next session, use another scenario before rereading your notes.
